Your after repair value (ARV) is the most important number in a flip. It decides how much you can offer, how much a lender will fund and whether you make money. Here's how to estimate it well.

## What is ARV?

ARV is what the property should sell for **after** your renovation, based on what similar renovated homes nearby have actually sold for.

## Step 1: Find comparable sales (comps)

Look for homes that have **sold** (not just listed) that are:

- **Close by:** ideally in the same neighborhood or subdivision
- **Recent:** sold within the last three to six months
- **Similar:** about the same square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, lot size and age
- **Renovated:** finished to the level you're planning

Three to five good comps are better than ten loose ones.

## Step 2: Adjust for differences

No two homes are identical. Adjust each comp up or down for differences like an extra bathroom, a garage, a bigger lot or a busier street. A real estate agent who knows the area is a huge help here.

## Step 3: Use price per square foot as a check

Divide each comp's sale price by its square footage, then apply a reasonable price per square foot to your property. If that number is far from your adjusted comps, dig deeper.

## Common ARV mistakes

- **Using list prices** instead of sold prices
- **Pulling comps from a nicer neighborhood** across a major road or school line
- **Comparing to a fully custom renovation** when your budget is for a standard one
- **Ignoring the market's direction;** if prices are softening, be conservative
- **Assuming the best-case number;** lenders and appraisers won't

## Why your lender cares about ARV

Many fix and flip lenders cap the total loan at a percentage of ARV, so an inflated ARV can mean a smaller loan than you planned, or a deal that doesn't work. A well-supported ARV makes your loan smoother.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between ARV and appraised value?

ARV is the estimated value after renovation, while an as-is appraisal values the property in its current condition. Lenders often look at both: the as-is value for the purchase and the ARV for the finished property.

### How many comps do I need to estimate ARV?

Three to five recent, nearby, similar sold properties is a good target. Quality matters more than quantity, so choose comps that truly match your finished property.

### How far back should comps go?

Ideally comps should be from the last three to six months. In a fast-changing market, the most recent sales matter most.

If you're flipping houses, you'll hear "hard money" a lot. Here's what it actually means, and what to look for before you sign anything.

## What is a hard money loan?

A hard money loan is a short-term loan for an investment property. Instead of focusing mainly on your income and tax returns like a bank mortgage, the lender looks hardest at **the deal itself**: what you're paying for the property, what the rehab will cost, and what it will be worth when it's done (the ARV, or after repair value).

That's why investors use them for fix and flips. They can close in days instead of weeks, and they can fund properties a bank won't touch until they're fixed up.

## How much will a lender give you?

Most fix & flip loans are sized in two parts:

- **A percentage of the purchase price.** For example, 80% of a $300,000 purchase is $240,000.
- **A percentage of the rehab budget,** often paid out in draws as the work gets done. For example, 100% of a $60,000 rehab is $60,000.

In that example the loan would be $300,000, and you'd bring the remaining $60,000 of the purchase price, plus closing costs, to the table.

## What does it cost?

Three things make up the cost of the money:

1. **Interest.** Usually interest only, paid monthly. On a $300,000 loan at 11%, that's about $2,750 a month.
2. **Origination points.** Paid at closing. One point is 1% of the loan, so $3,000 on our example.
3. **Time.** Every extra month you hold the property is another interest payment. A flip that takes 12 months instead of 8 costs four more payments.

## Questions to ask any lender

- How much of the purchase and the rehab will you fund?
- How do rehab draws work, and how fast are they paid?
- What are the rate and points, and are there any other fees?
- How long is the term, and what happens if I need an extension?
- How fast can you close?

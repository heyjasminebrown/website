Ask ten flippers how they decide what to offer and at least one will say "the 70% rule." It's a great starting point. It just isn't a finish line.

## What is the 70% rule?

The 70% rule says you shouldn't pay more than **70% of the after repair value (ARV), minus your rehab costs.**

**Maximum offer = (ARV × 70%) − rehab budget**

## An example

- ARV: $350,000
- Rehab budget: $55,000

($350,000 × 0.70) − $55,000 = **$190,000 maximum offer**

The 30% that's left over is meant to cover your costs and your profit.

## What the 70% rule leaves out

That 30% has to cover a lot more than people expect:

- **Financing costs:** origination points and monthly interest
- **Carrying costs:** interest payments, utilities, insurance and HOA dues for every month you hold the property
- **Closing costs to buy:** attorney and title fees, plus prorated taxes
- **Selling costs:** realtor fees and closing costs, which together are often 4% or more of the sale price
- **Surprises:** the rehab that runs over or the house that sits on the market an extra month

On a lower-priced property, those fixed costs eat a bigger share of the 30%, so the rule can make a thin deal look better than it is. On a higher-priced property, you may be able to pay a little more and still profit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the 70% rule include closing costs?

Not directly. The 70% rule only subtracts your rehab budget from 70% of the ARV. The remaining 30% is expected to cover closing costs, financing, carrying costs and your profit, so you need to check those separately.

### Is the 70% rule still realistic?

In competitive markets, many investors pay 75% or more of ARV and still profit, while others need to stay below 70% on lower-priced homes. Treat it as a quick screening tool, then run a full analysis on every deal.

### How do I calculate ARV for the 70% rule?

ARV is based on recent sold comparable properties nearby that match your property's size, condition and features after renovation. See my guide on [how to calculate ARV](/blog/how-to-calculate-arv).

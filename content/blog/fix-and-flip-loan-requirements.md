Fix and flip lenders care about two things: **is the deal good, and can you complete it?** Every requirement on their list ties back to one of those questions.

## 1. The deal

This matters most. Lenders look at:

- **Purchase price** compared with the property's as-is value
- **Rehab budget,** and whether it's realistic for the scope of work
- **ARV,** supported by recent sold comps
- **The spread,** meaning there's enough profit left after all costs to absorb surprises

A deal with a healthy margin can get funded even if you're newer. A thin deal is hard to fund no matter who you are.

## 2. Your experience

Lenders want to know you can finish the project. Past flips help, but if this is your first:

- Show the **contractor** you're using and their track record
- Bring in an **experienced partner** if you can
- Have a **detailed scope of work and timeline**

## 3. Credit

Most fix and flip lenders check credit, but it usually isn't the deciding factor it is for a mortgage. Stronger credit can mean better terms.

## 4. Cash and reserves

You'll need to show **proof of funds** for your down payment, closing costs and some months of payments.

## 5. Your entity

Most lenders lend to an **LLC** or other business entity rather than to you personally, so set yours up before you apply.

## Make your application stronger

- Use a [rehab budget worksheet](/free/rehab-budget) with line-item costs and contractor quotes
- Include three to five solid comps for your ARV
- Run the deal through a [fix & flip calculator](/calculator) and bring the numbers
- Keep your bank statements ready as proof of funds

## Frequently asked questions

### What credit score do I need for a fix and flip loan?

Requirements vary by lender. Many fix and flip lenders work with a wide range of credit scores because they focus on the deal, but higher scores generally get better rates and leverage.

### Do I need experience to get a fix and flip loan?

Not always. Many lenders fund first-time flippers, especially with a strong deal, a realistic budget and an experienced contractor or partner. Experienced investors may qualify for higher leverage or better rates.

### Can I get a fix and flip loan in my personal name?

Most fix and flip lenders require the loan to be in an LLC or other business entity, because these are business-purpose loans for investment properties.
